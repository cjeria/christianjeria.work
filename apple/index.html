Apple

Building internal tools and front-end solutions to streamline the industrial design product development process for Jony Ive's team.

Overview

For four years, I worked as a Human Interface Designer at Apple embedded within the Industrial Design group — Jony Ive's team. My focus was not on consumer-facing software, but on the internal tools and workflows that helped one of the world's most demanding design teams do their best work.

Working at this level meant operating at the intersection of design craft and engineering, understanding deeply how industrial designers think, and translating that into software that felt invisible — tools that got out of the way and let the work speak for itself.

The Problem

Apple's Industrial Design team is responsible for defining the physical form of every Apple product — from iPhone to MacBook to Apple Watch. Their process spans ideation, material exploration, model-making, and iterative refinement across dozens of concurrent projects.

The tooling that supported this process had not kept pace with the team's needs. Workflows were fragmented across disconnected systems, critical information lived in silos, and there was no unified way to track the status of a product from concept through to final design approval. The team needed bespoke software built with the same care they applied to hardware.

The Solution

I designed and helped build a suite of internal front-end solutions tailored specifically to how the Industrial Design team worked:

  • A web application to manage and track the product development lifecycle from early concept through design sign-off
  • A mobile app giving designers on-the-go access to project status, material specs, and cross-team updates
  • A set of front-end tools that integrated into existing workflows, reducing friction and eliminating manual hand-offs between teams

Every decision — from information architecture to interaction patterns to visual language — was made with the same precision the ID team brought to hardware. The software had to earn its place in one of the most design-conscious environments in the world.

The Impact

The tools shipped and became part of the team's daily workflow, meaningfully improving how one of Apple's most critical teams operated:

  • Unified fragmented processes into a single, coherent system for tracking product development
  • Reduced time spent on manual status updates and cross-team coordination
  • Gave designers a mobile-first way to stay connected to active projects without being tied to a desk
  • Established a design language for internal tooling that balanced Apple's aesthetic standards with the practical demands of a fast-moving team
